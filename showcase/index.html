Summary

LSA → BART
The Transformer is a novel neural network architecture relying entirely on attention mechanisms, dispensing with recurrence and convolutions. It achieves superior translation quality while being more parallelizable and requiring significantly less time to train than architectures based on recurrent or convolutional layers.

Proposed by Vaswani et al. at Google Brain in 2017, the model achieved 28.4 BLEU on English-to-German translation — outperforming all previously published results including ensembles. On English-to-French, it reached a new state-of-the-art of 41.0 BLEU, training in 3.5 days on 8 GPUs. The architecture uses stacked self-attention and pointwise fully connected layers for both encoder and decoder, with multi-head attention enabling simultaneous attention to different representational subspaces.

Methodology

The Transformer model uses an encoder-decoder structure. The encoder maps an input sequence of symbol representations to a sequence of continuous representations. Given this, the decoder generates an output sequence one symbol at a time in an auto-regressive fashion, consuming the previously generated symbols as additional input.

Both encoder and decoder are composed of stacks of N=6 identical layers. Each encoder layer has two sub-layers: a multi-head self-attention mechanism, and a position-wise fully connected feed-forward network. Each decoder layer has three sub-layers, the third of which performs multi-head attention over the encoder output.

Training used the Adam optimizer with β1=0.9, β2=0.98, and ε=10⁻⁹ with a custom learning rate schedule. Regularization applied dropout (P=0.1) to sub-layer outputs and label smoothing of ε=0.1. The base model was trained for 100,000 steps on 8 NVIDIA P100 GPUs for approximately 12 hours.

Results & Discussion

On WMT 2014 English-to-German translation, the big Transformer model outperforms the best previously reported models, including ensembles, by more than 2.0 BLEU, establishing a new state-of-the-art BLEU score of 28.4.

The big Transformer model achieved 41.0 BLEU on the WMT 2014 English-to-French translation task, outperforming all of the previously published single models, at less than 1/4 the training cost of the previous state-of-the-art model. Notably, the base model surpasses all previously published models and ensembles, at a fraction of the training cost of any of the competitive models.

Ablation studies revealed that reducing the attention key dimensionality hurts model quality, suggesting that determining compatibility requires a more sophisticated function than dot product. Models with larger dimensions and dropout perform better, and positional encoding with sinusoids varies per position and dimension without learned parameters.

Strengths & Weaknesses

Strengths: The Transformer's complete reliance on self-attention is a significant architectural contribution, enabling massively parallel training that was previously impossible with sequential RNN-based models. The multi-head attention mechanism allows the model to jointly attend to information from different representation subspaces — a powerful inductive bias. State-of-the-art BLEU scores at a fraction of the training cost of previous models is a compelling empirical result. The model also generalizes well to other tasks like English constituency parsing.

Weaknesses: Self-attention complexity is O(n²·d) with respect to sequence length, making it computationally expensive for very long sequences compared to convolutional approaches O(n/k layers). The model requires positional encodings since it has no inherent notion of sequence order, which is an engineering workaround rather than an architectural solution. Limited evaluation on tasks beyond machine translation weakens generalizability claims.

Future Research Directions

The authors explicitly identify extending the Transformer to problems involving input and output modalities other than text as a key direction — images, audio, and video. This prediction proved extraordinarily prescient: Vision Transformers (ViT), Audio Spectrogram Transformers, and multimodal models like CLIP and Flamingo all directly descended from this work.

Local, restricted attention mechanisms to efficiently handle large inputs such as images, audio and video are mentioned as a near-term research goal. This motivated Sparse Transformers, Longformer, BigBird, and other efficient attention variants. Making generation less sequential — particularly auto-regressive decoding — was also highlighted, inspiring non-autoregressive translation and diffusion-based language models.
